# Research Plan

For when the person is exploring a problem and doesn't know enough yet. The plan's job is to make the research small, fast, and pointed at a decision — because in slow orgs "we should do research" is how initiatives die. A good plan can be executed in one to two weeks with people the PM can actually reach.

## Structure

```
# Research plan — [initiative or question]

**Decision this research informs:** [the specific decision, and who makes it]
**Decide by:** [date]
**Owner:** [PM/designer]

## What we already know
- ...
## What we need to learn
1. [question]
2. ...
(3–5 questions. Each one, if answered, changes what we'd build.)

## Approach
| Question | Method | Who / source | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ... | ... | ... |

## Participants and access
- Primary users: [who, how many, how we reach them]
- Proxies if we can't reach primary users: [call-center reps, client-service managers, implementation consultants, support tickets, recorded calls]
- Internal experts: [ops, compliance, the person who built the current thing]

## Timeline
| Week | Activity |
|---|---|

## Output
[What the research produces: e.g. a one-page findings summary + a recommended reading of the problem, delivered to (decider) on (date).]

## Constraints and notes
- ...
```

## What good looks like

**Decision first.** Everything hangs off "what decision does this inform?" If the person can't name one, the honest answer is that they need a one-pager first, not research; say so in the read. Research without a decision attached produces a slide deck nobody opens.

**Questions, not topics.** "Understand the enrollment experience" is a topic. "At which step do new hires stop, and what were they trying to figure out when they stopped?" is a question. Each question should be phrased so you'd know when you had the answer.

**Methods that fit the access they actually have.** In enterprise B2B2C environments (retirement plans, benefits, insurance) the PM often can't talk to end users directly — the client owns the relationship. Plan around that: recorded support calls, ticket analysis, call-center shadowing, client-facing staff interviews, and existing analytics come before "recruit 8 participants." Note what would require client or compliance sign-off (recording, contacting participants) and propose the path that doesn't.

**Effort is honest.** Small numbers. Five conversations beat a survey that takes three weeks to get approved.

**Output is a decision aid.** Name the form (one page, findings + recommendation) and who receives it. Findings that don't land on the decider's desk didn't happen.

## Traps

- A plan that needs a research ops team the person doesn't have.
- Skipping the proxies. Internal people who talk to users every day are the fastest signal available and usually underused.
- Ten research questions. Pick the three that would most change the build.
- No date. Without "decide by," the research expands to fill the quarter.
