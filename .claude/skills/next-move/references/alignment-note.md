# Alignment Note

For when there are too many threads and nobody's aligned — after a meeting that went sideways, or when three stakeholders each believe a different thing was agreed. The note is short, sent the same day, and written so that silence counts as agreement. It is the cheapest tool for creating shared reality in an org that doesn't write things down.

## Structure

```
**Subject:** [Initiative] — where we are and what's open (as of [date])

Quick note to make sure we're all working from the same picture. If anything below is wrong, reply by [day] — otherwise I'll treat this as our shared understanding.

**What we've agreed**
- ...
- ...

**What's still open**
| Open item | Owner | Needed by |
|---|---|---|
| ... | ... | ... |

**What I'm doing next**
- ...

**Next checkpoint:** [meeting or date], where we'll [close X / decide Y].
```

## What good looks like

**Sent within hours, not days.** The value decays fast; by tomorrow everyone's memory of the meeting has diverged further.

**Agreements are stated as facts, plainly.** "We agreed the first release covers new-hire enrollment only; existing-participant changes are a later phase." If the PM isn't sure something was agreed, it goes in open items, not agreements — but they should be brave about what actually was agreed. Under-claiming is as unhelpful as over-claiming.

**Every open item has one owner and a date.** Not a team, a person. Not "soon," a day. If nobody can be named, the open item is really "who owns this?" and that's what should be written.

**The silence-is-agreement line.** It's the mechanism. It shifts the burden from "get everyone to confirm" (impossible in slow orgs) to "give everyone a chance to object" (works). Keep it polite and keep the deadline short — two business days.

**Fits in an email without scrolling.** If it doesn't, it's two notes or it's a one-pager.

**Names the next checkpoint and what it's for.** A meeting with a purpose gets attended.

## Roadmap-archaeology variant

When the misalignment is that some people expect a roadmap item to ship and nobody can say why, the "What we've agreed" section may be nearly empty. That's fine and honest. Put the real open item first: "Original rationale for [item] — owner: [whoever added it or their successor] — needed by [date]. If we can't recover it, I'll draft a reconstructed one-pager and we decide build / rescope / retire on [date]." That converts an awkward hallway conversation into a scheduled decision.

## Traps

- Turning it into meeting minutes. Nobody reads minutes. This is agreements, open items, next steps — nothing else.
- Hedging every agreement ("I think we might have agreed…"). State it; let people correct it.
- Open items with no owner. That's how they stay open.
- Sending it to twenty people. Send to the people in the room plus the one person who should have been.
